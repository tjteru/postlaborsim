Methodology & Assumptions

▼

Monte Carlo Approach

This simulation uses Monte Carlo methods to model the economic impact of AI adoption across sectors. The model incorporates stochastic variables for adoption rates, productivity gains, and employment displacement effects.

Key Model Equations

  • GDP Growth: ΔY = α × A × P × (1 + ε × L)
  • Employment Change: ΔL = -D × E × A × (1 + η)
  • Productivity Impact: P = base_productivity × multiplier × exposure
  • Sectoral Aggregation: Total = Σ(sector_weight × sector_impact)

Parameter Definitions

  • A: AI Adoption Rate (5%-80%)
  • D: Job Displacement Rate (5%-40%)
  • P: Productivity Multiplier (0.5x-2.0x)
  • ε: Demand Elasticity (-1.5 to -0.3)
  • E: Sector AI Exposure (sector-specific)
  • η: Random shock (normally distributed)
AI Adoption Rate

Percentage of workforce tasks that will be affected by AI over the time horizon. Higher values simulate rapid AI integration across the economy.

Range: 5% (minimal adoption) to 80% (widespread transformation)

Annual TFP Growth Rate

Total Factor Productivity measures how efficiently the economy converts inputs (labor, capital) into outputs. AI can significantly boost TFP through automation and optimization.

Range: 0.05% (modest) to 0.8% (transformative)

Job Displacement Rate

Percentage of jobs directly lost to AI automation. The model accounts for both job losses and new job creation from economic growth and emerging roles.

Range: 5% (minimal disruption) to 40% (severe displacement)

Monte Carlo Iterations

Number of simulation runs to perform. More iterations provide more accurate statistical estimates but take longer to compute. 500-1000 iterations typically provide reliable results.

Range: 100 (quick estimate) to 2,000 (high precision)

Productivity Multiplier

Amplification factor for AI-driven productivity gains in each sector. Values above 1.0 indicate AI enhances worker output beyond base expectations.

Range: 0.5x (conservative) to 2.0x (highly optimistic)

Demand Elasticity

Consumer responsiveness to price changes from productivity gains. More negative values mean consumers are more sensitive to prices, affecting how much GDP growth translates from productivity improvements.

Range: -1.5 (highly elastic) to -0.3 (inelastic)

Time Horizon

Number of years to project into the future. Longer horizons allow compounding effects but introduce more uncertainty. Results show cumulative impact over this period.

Range: 5 years (near-term) to 15 years (long-term)
